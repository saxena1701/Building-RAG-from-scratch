# De-escalation with Angry Customers

*Last updated: 2024-09-15*

## Goals

1. Reduce the customer's emotional intensity
2. Understand the actual problem
3. Solve the problem
4. Preserve the relationship if possible

## Don't

- Don't take it personally.
- Don't match the customer's emotional intensity.
- Don't argue about whether they have a right to be upset.
- Don't promise things you can't deliver.

## Do

- Acknowledge the frustration without agreeing or disagreeing with the cause: "I can hear that this has been frustrating, and I'm going to do my best to help."
- Use the customer's name (sparingly).
- Ask open questions to understand: "Can you walk me through what happened?"
- Summarize back: "So let me make sure I understand..."
- Move to solution when the customer signals readiness (often a calming in voice or pacing).

## When to Disengage

Customers may cross lines into abuse:
- Personal insults toward you
- Threats (physical, legal, social media)
- Profanity directed at you

For abuse:
1. Warn once: "I want to help you and I will, but I need us to keep this conversation respectful."
2. If continues, disconnect: "I'm going to end this call now. You can contact us again when ready, or reach a supervisor at..." (and provide path).
3. Document the interaction.

## Self-Care

Take a break after a difficult call. These are emotionally taxing. Speak with your team lead if a call is sticking with you.
